# Professional Templates

Use these domain lenses to adapt the shared Project and Milestone model. They are guidance for shaping outcomes and Acceptance Criteria, not separate Skills or rigid forms.

The common model remains unchanged: Project Goal → Roadmap → Milestones → evidence-based review. Select only the template guidance relevant to the current Project. Do not force every Project into one of the examples; for another domain, derive a suitable outcome and verification method.

## 1. Software

### Outcome lens

A Software Milestone should produce a usable, testable capability or a verified technical foundation needed by a later outcome. Prefer vertical slices and end-to-end behavior over component completion alone.

### Useful Acceptance Criteria

Depending on the Milestone:
- the intended user or system behavior works in a reproducible environment;
- critical integration paths work end to end;
- relevant automated tests pass and their scope is clear;
- security, data integrity, compatibility, or performance constraints are verified where material;
- operational or migration requirements are addressed when the outcome depends on them.

### Common planning risks

- counting a UI or API stub as a complete user capability;
- treating code written as proof that behavior works;
- deferring integration until the end;
- allowing optional polish to block the minimum usable outcome.

## 2. Learning

### Outcome lens

A Learning Milestone should describe a capability the learner can demonstrate, not merely a chapter, video, or number of study hours completed.

### Useful Acceptance Criteria

Depending on the learning goal:
- explain the concept accurately in the learner's own words;
- apply it to a new problem, not only repeat a worked example;
- complete a practical exercise or project;
- identify limitations, trade-offs, or common failure modes;
- retrieve or use the knowledge after an appropriate interval when retention matters.

### Common planning risks

- equating content consumption with mastery;
- advancing because a calendar date passed;
- creating too many topics without a practical outcome;
- using an assessment that only tests memorization when the goal is application.

## 3. Research

### Outcome lens

A Research Milestone should reduce a defined uncertainty or produce a conclusion that is supported by evidence. The result may be a decision, a model, a comparison, or a documented finding.

### Useful Acceptance Criteria

Depending on the research question:
- the question and scope are explicit;
- sources or data are relevant and their quality is assessed;
- the method and assumptions are documented;
- important counter-evidence and alternative explanations are considered;
- conclusions are traceable to evidence;
- limitations and unresolved questions are stated;
- another reader can understand how the conclusion was reached.

### Common planning risks

- measuring progress by the number of papers or pages read;
- treating an interesting hypothesis as a verified conclusion;
- ignoring disconfirming evidence;
- continuing research after the decision-relevant uncertainty has already been reduced enough.

## 4. Content

### Outcome lens

A Content Milestone should produce a defined deliverable for a target audience and purpose. A draft, reviewed asset, and published item are different states.

### Useful Acceptance Criteria

Depending on the deliverable:
- audience, purpose, format, and key message are clear;
- the draft meets the agreed content scope;
- factual claims and attributions are checked where needed;
- editorial, accessibility, and format requirements are met;
- the final asset is approved;
- publication or delivery is verified when publication is part of the outcome.

### Common planning risks

- treating a draft as a published outcome;
- adding new topics that dilute the central message;
- over-polishing before validating the audience and purpose;
- failing to distinguish creation, review, approval, and publication.

## 5. Choosing or adapting a template

1. Identify the Project's real outcome and the kind of evidence that can demonstrate it.
2. Select the closest domain lens; a Project may combine more than one.
3. Keep the common Milestone fields and lifecycle.
4. Add domain-specific Acceptance Criteria only when they improve verification.
5. Avoid adding fields or process steps that do not change a decision.
6. If the domain is unfamiliar or the evidence standard is uncertain, state the assumption and propose a small validation rather than inventing a universal rule.
