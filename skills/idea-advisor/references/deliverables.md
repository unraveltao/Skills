# Design deliverables

Choose the smallest set of artifacts that makes the decision clear and usable. A well-structured answer can be enough; separate files are optional.

## Problem definition

Capture the user or audience, context, problem, desired outcome, constraints, and important unknowns. Avoid embedding a favored solution in the problem statement.

## Options comparison

Compare only viable and meaningfully different approaches. Tie tradeoffs to user priorities. Include evidence quality, implementation/operating cost, risks, dependencies, and a recommendation. Do not imply false precision with unsupported scores.

## Lightweight PRD

When requirements help, include: context and goal; target users and jobs; in-scope capabilities; non-goals; constraints and assumptions; user-visible behavior; acceptance criteria; risks and open questions. Keep acceptance criteria observable and testable. Do not prescribe architecture without a reason.

## Decision record

Use the schema and history rules in [decision management](decision-management.md) and [decision record template](../templates/decision-record.md).

## Implementation handoff

When another agent or team will implement, provide the agreed direction, problem, scope, non-goals, behavior, acceptance criteria, evidence and assumptions, constraints, relevant decision IDs, open questions, and verification expectations. Keep implementation tasks out unless explicitly requested. Use [design handoff template](../templates/design-handoff.md).

## Quality check

Before delivery, confirm that the proposal answers the actual question; evidence and assumptions are distinguishable; the recommendation follows the user's criteria; alternatives and risks are candid; acceptance criteria can be checked; and unresolved decisions are explicit. A missing answer is acceptable when marked as an open question rather than silently invented.
