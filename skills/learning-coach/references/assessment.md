# Assessment

## Responsibility and handoff

Own evidence-based diagnosis of specified learning outcomes. Do not reteach the lesson, run the learner's project or silently update persistent progress. Provide specific findings to learning workflow when saving is requested or agreed, and to teaching/practice as remediation targets.

Input: outcomes from curriculum, learner answers or inspected artifacts from teaching/practice, and assessment scope. Output: outcome-level findings with the observed evidence, limits, and a specific gap or next check. Do not convert missing evidence into a negative judgment.

## Assessment

Select questions against the learner's goal and distinguish recall, mechanism and transfer. Use fresh scenarios rather than repeating lesson questions, and combine explanation, code analysis or practice as appropriate. Pose the questions and wait for the learner's answers before grading; a requested answer key may be supplied separately.

Compare each actual answer or inspected artifact with expected reasoning, explain errors and missing evidence, and identify specific gaps. Track outcomes as Verified, Unverified or Needs reinforcement with evidence. One easy correct answer is insufficient to establish transfer. Recommend focused review, then use a new question or practice task to recheck. Lack of tool access is not evidence of inability. Record assessment and practice evidence, including limits, only when the learner requests progress saved; do not claim an outcome before evidence exists.

If several observations suggest a teaching rule may be ineffective, provide the concrete evidence and uncertainty to [evolution](evolution.md). Assessment diagnoses learner outcomes; Evolution decides whether the evidence supports a shared-rule proposal. An individual learner gap alone is not evidence that the general rule is defective.
